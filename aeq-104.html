Product Description

A worn 175 rpm brush does not stop working — it starts working badly, which is harder to notice. The bristle tips polish over and splay outward, so the brush stops biting the coating and begins to skip. On a 175 rpm machine that shows up as swirl marks in the finish, stripes where the deck hopped, and edges you cannot get clean without leaning on the handle. More chemical and more passes only wear the floor; the brush is the part that has failed. AEQ-104B is the replacement rotary floor brush for that job, dropped back onto the same centre hub and ring plate.

Two sizes cover the decks we build and the decks already in service. AEQ-104B (17 in / 432 mm) is the standard 17 inch single-disc brush; AEQ-104A (18 in / 457 mm) fits 18 inch decks. Match the brush to the deck it runs on, because a larger brush on a smaller machine fouls the skirt and a smaller one leaves an unscrubbed ring exactly where you overlap your passes. If your machine is a 154 rpm slow-speed scrubber rather than a 175 rpm floor machine, take the 154 rpm floor brush AEQ-101 instead — the bristle is filled for that lower turning speed.

The bristle is dense and cut to an even length right to the rim, which is what keeps a 175 rpm deck running flat instead of hopping; a cheap brush with uneven tufts is the usual cause of swirl marks on a fresh coat. Medium-stiff black bristle bites coating and grime without gouging the surface, so it handles black stripping pads of work on a coated floor as well as a plain detergent scrub. The body is a black moulded disc with a ring plate held by six screws and a centre hub collar, so the load sits on a true centre and the brush runs without vibration at speed. Use it for a detergent scrub on concrete, tile, terrazzo, vinyl or epoxy, for stripping old finish before a recoat, and for prep before sealer goes down.
