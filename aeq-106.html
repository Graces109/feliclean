Product Description

A pad that slips does not look like a pad that slips. At 175 rpm the machine keeps turning, the operator keeps walking, and the floor still comes out streaked because the pad is no longer being driven at the speed the deck is running. The usual cause is the holder: once the stud grid wears down, the pad drags on the disc face instead of locking to it, so it turns slower than the machine, frays at the edge, and polishes unevenly. AEQ-106B is the replacement pad driver for that. Fit it on the same hub screws and the pad is gripped from the centre again.

Two sizes cover the decks we build and the decks already in service. AEQ-106B (17 in / 432 mm) is the standard 17 inch pad holder; AEQ-106A (18 in / 457 mm) fits 18 inch decks. Buy the holder to the pad: a 17 inch pad on an 18 inch holder leaves a bare ring that does the work instead of the pad, and a 17 inch holder under an 18 inch pad will not hold the outer edge, which is where a buffing pad lifts first. If the machine is a 154 rpm slow-speed scrubber rather than a 175 rpm machine, take the 154 rpm pad holder AEQ-103 — it is sized for that lower turning speed.

The face is a moulded stud grid, so a standard pad grips on contact without velcro, glue or a backing disc, and the studs run evenly out to the rim so the pad sits flat and cannot cup in the middle under load. Slots cut through the face let solution and soil pass instead of collecting under the pad, and the ribs on the back keep the disc square when the machine is leaned into a corner. That matters more at 175 rpm than at low speed: a holder that runs off centre at buffing speed leaves swirl marks on a fresh coat, so the steel hub is screwed down onto a true centre rather than pressed in. Use it for scrubbing with red or tan pads, spray buffing and polishing with white or beige pads, and stripping with black pads, on concrete, ceramic tile, terrazzo and coated floors.
