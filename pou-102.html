Product Description

The POU-102 is the container a lorry lifts. At 660 L and 1100 L on a 126 × 76 × 123 cm body it is the unit a housing estate, a market or a municipal route puts out at a collection point, fills for a week and has emptied by the truck rather than by hand. It is not a bigger wheelie bin; it is the size at which waste stops being carried and starts being collected.

Both capacities share the body footprint. That is deliberate: a collection lorry's lifting gear is set for one dimension, so a route can run 660 L containers at flats and 1100 L at a market on the same vehicle, and a site can add capacity without re-rigging. The four castors sit under the load, which is the only way one person moves a full 660 L container across a yard to the kerb.

The lid is hinged to the body rather than loose: it opens for a bag, a barrow or a sack, it stays with the container in wind, and it keeps rain out of waste that sits outdoors between collections — which is what stops a container turning into a water butt and a weight problem on collection day.

Green, blue, red and grey are the colours, in the mould rather than on a label: a municipality or an estate assigns each colour to a stream — general waste, mixed recycling, glass, organics — and the container itself carries the system on the street. The body is one moulding, so a forklift, a wet yard and a hose-down do not split a seam or start a rust line.
