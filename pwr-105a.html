The PWR-105A is the smallest single bucket in our wringer trolley range at 20 litres, and it is built for the floors a big trolley never reaches: guest bathrooms, clinic treatment rooms, office washrooms, small canteens and the aisles between desks. At 40 × 27 × 34 cm it passes through a doorway with the mop still in the bucket, and it stands low enough that you can work around it in a room the size of a hotel bathroom. The fill is 20 litres, which is roughly a morning of bathroom mopping before it needs emptying.

Wringing is by press bar rather than a hook-on basket. The mop goes into the wringer housing at the rear, you push the stainless steel bar down and the ribbed PP plate presses the water out in one stroke; the torsion spring lifts the bar back up so the next mop loads without you pulling it. The bar is long enough to keep both hands out of the dirty water, and the ABS grip stays comfortable when the shift is long. Nothing clips on or off between buckets, which is what makes this model quick to move from room to room.

The removable centre partition is the detail buyers ask about most. It splits the 20 litres into a dirty zone at the wringing end and a clean zone at the front, so the mop you are mopping with is not standing in the water you have just squeezed out. When the job is done the partition lifts out with one hand and the bucket rinses as a plain single bucket. Sites that do not want it can order the same trolley without the partition — the moulded slots stay covered.

The body is thickened injection-moulded PP: no rust, no paint to chip, and it takes the bathroom, kitchen and chlorine-based detergents used in commercial cleaning. Four iron castors carry the load, turn on the spot in a corridor or in front of a sink, and roll over tiled thresholds and lift lips without the bucket tipping. Overall 40 × 27 × 34 cm; one piece packs into a 46.5 × 27 × 29 cm carton, so a pallet holds a useful trial quantity and courier costs stay predictable for samples. Yellow is stocked and ships from Guangzhou.
